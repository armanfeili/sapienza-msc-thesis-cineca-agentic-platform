Follow this structure for the thesis:
- Title
- Abstract
- Introduction
- Related works
- Proposed solution
- Tests
- Results
- Conclusions
- Future works
- Appendices
- Bibliography

Sometimes some sections are grouped together, use this structure as a basis and try to stick with it. While you work on your thesis, see if it's good to follow this structure, or if you should change it a bit.

###############################################

Reading your thesis what should be clear is:
- what is the problem that we're dealing with
- what have you done to address to problem
- what kind of results you've gotten

Everything else that you write should help better explain this things and make your thesis richer.
If something doesn't help explain, or worse makes the thesis less clear, then it's probably best to leave it out.

Specific implementation details are not necessary.
Usually the most that is required about implementation details is the hardware used and the software version of the main libraries used.

Assume that whoever is going to read your thesis doesn't know anything about what you've done, and probably doesn't even understand it; don't make too many assumptions and write everything as clear as possible.
I'm not telling you to assume that the reader is 5 years old, because people in universities are a bit smarter than that, but almost 5.


###############################################

Hints to improve your thesis:
- Group up sections and chapters
- Remove implementation details

GROUP UP SECTIONS AND CHAPTERS
The structure of the thesis isn't something that cannot be changed, but it should be coherent.
Giving a good structure is important.

For instance, a thorough explanation of your work/methodology should go under a chapter called about the "proposed method", and everything should be there.
Another actual example, in your draft you have a bunch of appendices. They should all be under a single chapter called "Appendices", which not only groups them up, but make it clear that there you will find the appendices.
While you have created a bunch of chapters, one for each appendix, and with a name that doesn't help in recognizing them as appendices, unless when goes to the specific page and reads "Appendix A".


REMOVE IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
The most important thing is to explain how your method works, in such a way that, if someone wants to, it is replicable.

Explaining every single implementation detail is too much:
- it isn't helpful in explaining the methodology
- makes the thesis harder to read, because it's easier getting lost
- makes the thesis longer, which might end up being too long

For instance, I noticed that you even mentioned which python file implements some specific stuff.
That is a perfect example of a detail that can be left out of the final thesis (but good for a technical report, which might be suited for CINECA).

There isn't a specific amount of pages required, but the length must be in some way justified.


OTHER HINTS

I noticed that you used a lot of bulleted lists (i.e. \begin{itemize}).
Those are helpful, but shouldn't be used everywhere.

A rule of thumb is to use them to list the main aspects of some section/subsection, that you are going to later explain with normal sentences, without lists.

Small lists of non-important stuff can either be:
- left out, if something is not even important enough to be mentioned
- written in a small list in normal text, without an explicit list; e.g. "Programs can be classified in compiled and interpreted"
